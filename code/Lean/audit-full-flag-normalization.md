# Full-flag convolution and the elementary normalization

30 October 2026. This records the replacement of the former arbitrary-chain
convolution hypothesis by the full-flag Hecke realization. The construction
uses actual polynomial Laurent lattices and actual configurations throughout.
The integrated build and final theorem dependency checks are recorded in
`reports/verification.json`.

## The external theorem

`FullFlagHeckeConvolution.Input K p hp` has two fields:

- `orbits`: a pair of full, degree-normalized periodic flags has a unique
  relative position in the balanced affine window group. Relative position
  is defined by a common invertible Laurent-polynomial linear map carrying
  the literal coordinate modules and window translates to the two flags.
- `convolution`: evaluation of a product in the presented Hecke algebra is
  binary convolution of these full-flag kernels.

These are the full-flag affine Bruhat/Hecke realization, in the degree-zero
component. Sources are Lusztig, *Aperiodicity in quantum affine gl_n*,
§§1.3–1.9, especially Lemmas 1.4–1.5 and §1.7; and Cui, §7.1, Lemma 7.1.
Lusztig's lattices are polynomial lattices. The project's proved
`PolynomialLattice.exists_basis_extending` supplies his literal basis
condition. The full flags have absolute degree i at node i, including the
cyclic seam. This is the balanced component of the source's extended affine
indexing, not an assertion about all extended windows.

The field is a precise formulation of this major theorem in the project's
objects, not a verbatim quotation of Cui. It includes no partial-gallery
count, finite parabolic Poincare count, six-map word, canonical correction,
or special-family coefficient. The theorem itself is not proved here.

## Objects and proved deductions

`PolynomialFullGallery.FullFlag` retains every node. `forget I` literally
restricts the ordered cyclic flag to `retained I`; `Lift F` is the subtype
of full flags whose restriction equals the given partial flag. These are
configuration types, independently of their cardinalities.

1. `ParabolicFlagStabilizer` proves that retaining the same coordinate cuts
   is equivalent to membership in the subgroup generated by I. A common
   Laurent-linear map is canceled in `FullFlagHeckeConvolution.forget_eq_iff`.
   Thus the parabolic agreement test is proved, not part of orbit input.
2. The finite standard sum, eigenvalues, absorption and nested spherical
   ideal inclusion are proved in the parabolic modules. Consequently the
   kernel of the actual canonical symmetrizer C_I is the retained-cut
   indicator multiplied by v^length(w_I).
3. `FiniteFlagExtension` fills missing ranks of an actual finite chain of
   subspaces. `LatticeFlagUnroll`, `LatticeFlagCompletePeriod`, and
   `LatticeFlagExtension` perform this in a one-period quotient, then fold
   back at the original cyclic cut with the original degrees.
   `PolynomialFlagExtension.lift_nonempty` algebraizes that full lift.
   This works over arbitrary fields and does not infer existence from a
   presumed positive count.
4. The binary convolution theorem applied to C_I squared, together with
   the proved absorption identity C_I squared = h_I C_I, counts the actual
   lift fiber. `PolynomialConvolutionNormalization.scalar_specialize`
   proves h_I = v^length(w_I) P_I(Q), where P_I(Q) is the sum of
   Q^length(a) over the actual finite subgroup. Cancellation gives
   `PolynomialFullLiftCardinality.lift_card_eq` for every partial flag.
5. `PolynomialFullGallery.configurationEquiv` identifies a full-flag chain
   with a partial gallery together with an independent full lift of each
   common-facet refinement. The map remembers actual lattices, and its
   inverse reconstructs the full middle flags. Counting this equivalence
   gives the product of the Poincare factors.
6. `FullFlagHeckeConvolution.numerator_coordinate_count` uses binary
   convolution inductively to count the full-gallery type for the actual
   symmetrizer product. `normalized_raw_of_fullGallery_count` then applies
   the already proved integral cleared identity and cancels its specialized
   nonzero denominator.
7. `PolynomialCanonicalFullFlags` proves that the complete coordinate
   endpoints forget to the original canonical partial endpoints, with the
   same window. The subsequent completed-lattice comparison transports
   the same configurations; it does not change their indexing.

All sums used to count configurations have proved finite support. Classical
choice constructs bases and equivalences; no compiled decision oracle
supplies a counting theorem.

## The normalization checked in Lean

Let Q=p² and v=1/p. The normalized Hecke element is H_w=v^length(w) T_w.
Thus its coefficient is multiplied by v^length(w) when interpreted as an
orbit-indicator kernel. If the successive masks are I_0,...,I_r and
R_a=I_(a-1) intersection I_a, the numerator contributes
v^(sum length(w_Ia)), while the denominator contributes
v^(sum length(w_Ra)) times the product of P_Ra(Q).

The actual configuration bijection gives exactly that product as the number
of full lifts of each partial gallery. The remaining exponent is
`Chain.weight - length(w)`. The coefficient is valid for every affine w,
not only double-coset maxima. The target is the left kernel endpoint and
the source the right. No ordinary/spherical/antispherical exchange occurs.

The original integral maps are constructed before specialization. Division
occurs only in the rational finite-field calculation, by factors proved
nonzero. The canonical-to-standard coefficients and the characteristic
transfer retain their original conventions.

## Limits

This proves the elementary passage from the major full-flag realization to
the partial-gallery coefficient formula. It does not formalize the affine
Bruhat/Hecke realization itself, canonical-basis existence or positivity,
or the representation-theoretic transfer. Their explicit hypotheses remain
in `literature-inputs.md`; no unconditional Lean counterexample is asserted.
