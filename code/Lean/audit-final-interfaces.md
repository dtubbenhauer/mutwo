## Findings

I found no mathematical mismatch in the inspected interfaces and no special
family count, support classification, canonical correction, row sum or
cohomology dimension hidden inside `WebLiterature`. The representation
transfer now has named modules and general hypotheses, rather than an
uninterpreted numerical coefficient assigned to an arbitrary module.

Two qualifications must remain visible in the final status:

1. The completed Laurent-series realization is a general unformalized
   comparison, in addition to the statements literally written in Cui's
   polynomial Laurent model. It is not a new original-family lemma, but it
   is more than quoting Cui's theorem number without explaining the model.
2. `AndersenAJS` combines several general theorems and comparisons: eventual
   validity of the character formula, Andersen's degree-one interpretation,
   the ordinary/spherical KL dictionary, and the regular-cochain model of
   rational cohomology. It is an explicit theorem hypothesis, not a theorem
   already constructed in mathlib. The same distinction applies to `BNP`.

At this snapshot `row_sum_of_contribution_bounds` still displays its three
original contribution-bound premises. They are not fields of
`WebLiterature`. A final theorem should discharge them with the new original
proofs before being described as complete modulo literature. An unconditional
Lean theorem requires construction of the literature bundles as well.

## Web framework

The actual Hecke algebra is presented by the affine quadratic and braid
relations. Its standard basis is tied to reduced words. Its KL basis is tied
to bar invariance, triangularity and ordinary Bruhat support. The polynomial
normalization is `v^(ell(w)-ell(y))*P(y,w)(v^-2)`; no special KL value is a
field of these structures.

All parabolics are actual subgroups generated by specified simple
reflections. `LongestData`, parabolic symmetrizer laws, corner compatibility,
standard refinement, cyclic block lengths, and diagram invariance range over
general finite parabolics or diagram automorphisms. The refinement theorem
uses actual finite double-coset sums, not a matrix declared to be an operator.

The generic matrix-length converse is legitimate: a finite-parabolic double
coset has a unique longest element; the orbit dimension fixes its length.
An element of that coset attaining that length is the longest element.
The project separately proves the original matrix coarsening and dimension
identities before using this criterion.

The counting parameter and Hecke parameter remain separate. Over a field
of order `p^2`, the specialization is `v=1/p`, so the counting parameter is
`Q=v^-2`. The source is fixed throughout. The exponent is calculated from
actual parabolic longest lengths and the actual endpoint length. Prime
specialization interpolation is proved internally, so the generic
convolution input does not also assume the desired polynomial identity.

The supporting general matrix and convolution statements are in
[Cui, §7.1, Lemmas 7.1–7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1407.4557v3), together with
[Du–Fu, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.1868). The row inequality in the
periodic dimension is `i >= k`, exactly as in the Lean sum.

## Completed lattices

The lattice object is an actual finitely generated `k[[t]]`-submodule of
`k((t))^N` spanning the ambient Laurent vector space. Flags record actual
ordered lattice nodes, cyclic containment and relative degrees. This is
stronger than an arbitrary collection of stable vector subspaces.

The additional generic comparison has a concrete mathematical explanation:
bounded lattice intervals are determined by submodules of
`t^-a k[[t]]^N / t^b k[[t]]^N`, which is the same finite module obtained from
polynomial lattices. For a finite chain all relevant incidences are bounded
in such a quotient. A common Laurent-linear coordinate change transports
the endpoint pair to the monomial coordinate pair. This explains why the
finite flag counts and normalizations agree with the polynomial model.
The requisite boundedness/completion theorem is currently part of the
explicit general framework input; that explanation is not a Lean proof of it.

## Fixed trivial source and the module family

The application uses `b=u^-1`, proves `lambda=b dot_p 0`, and preserves the
ordinary lower index `w0`. Simultaneous inversion identifies the required
upper index with `w0*b`. The source in the cohomological interpretation is
therefore the trivial module. The argument never substitutes arbitrary
simple-to-simple Ext dimensions for first cohomology.

`SimpleModule` consists of an actual representation of `SL_N(k)`, an actual
nonzero highest-weight vector, irreducibility, finite dimension, and
polynomial matrix coefficients. `RationalH1` is regular crossed
homomorphisms modulo genuine coboundaries. All literature applications use
an algebraic closure of `F_p`; they do not infer rational algebraic-group
structure from arbitrary functions on finite-field points.

The regular-cochain interpretation agrees with
[Jantzen, 1985 preprint, §§4.14–4.16, Proposition 4.16](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/607/1/preprint_1985_9.pdf)
(printed page 79). The comparison with derived rational cohomology remains
inside the general representation-theoretic input.

The AJS threshold depends on the rank and is independent of the separately
imposed BNP bound. `p>N` verifies the family weights' dominance,
restrictedness and regularity; it does not stand in for the character
formula. The source conditions match the degree-one formulation in
[Lübeck, Theorem 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.07055v2).

I also rechecked the original BNP page 141 image in
`RevisedWeb/investigation/root/bnp-transfer-page141.png`. The substitution
`s=1,r=1,nu=0,u=b` gives the prime-field comparison under `p>=5(N-1)`.
The formal input asserts a vector-space isomorphism, without claiming that
an unspecified comparison is literally a particular cocycle map. See
[Bendel–Nakano–Pillen, §7.5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-4049(01)00024-X).

## PSL, faithfulness and absolute irreducibility

These last steps are actual proofs, not transfer hypotheses:

- Sum-zero integral highest-weight coordinates make every scalar torus
  element act trivially on the highest-weight vector. Its fixed space is
  invariant under the whole group, so irreducibility gives trivial central
  action on the entire module.
- The finite center injects into `F_p`'s unit group. Its order is positive
  and less than `p`, hence nonzero in the coefficient field. Its first
  cohomology vanishes. The actual inflation–restriction sequence, including
  the identification of invariants with the descended coefficient module,
  gives equality of the SL and PSL first-cohomology dimensions.
- Steinberg restriction and separation of the zero restricted weight are
  explicit general inputs. They give a nontrivial irreducible finite
  restriction. PSL simplicity is also explicit. Nontriviality then implies
  faithfulness by the proved kernel argument.
- Absolute irreducibility uses the actual tensor-product scalar extension.
  Over the chosen algebraically closed field, the algebraic-closure scalar
  map is an isomorphism; the resulting irreducibility proof is internal.

The target quantifies over all fields, all finite groups and faithful
finite-dimensional absolutely irreducible modules. The constructed family
lies in that class. The argument permits both rank and characteristic to
vary; it does not establish unboundedness at a fixed characteristic. Its
row-sum consequence is an existence lower bound for some member of each
row, not the individual conjectural formula `mu_(j;m)=j`.

## Review of the final contribution margins

The inspected `MainContributionSupport` and `MainContributionBounds` follow
the manuscript's six-type argument. In the notation `l=m-rank`, the full,
strong and weak allowances are respectively `2*l-3`, `l-1` and `l-2`.
Negative allowances force the actual canonical multiplicity to vanish.
Corank zero is handled separately and identifies only the terminal marked
family; it is not fed into a positive-corank estimate.

At a marked standard of rank j, a spherical rank-s contribution has margin
`2*(s-j)+6`; lower ranks give zero. Strong and weak one-line terms have margins
`2*(s-j)+2` and `2*(s-j)+4`, including exact zero for the invalid rank range.
`OneLineMargins` proves the sharp statements, and the final assembly uses
the weaker degree-at-least-one consequence because only the constant term
is being read. This loses no information needed by the manuscript's proof.

At every spherical standard Z_r, one-line products have degree at least
three (and vanish for auxiliary rank at most r). Marked terms use ordinary
strict KL triangularity after their surviving multiplicity is proved one.
The full type is identified with an actual Z_s, and its allowance forces
`s<m-1`, so neither terminal spherical rank is silently omitted.

At the fixed source w0, the strong and weak minimum degrees are `l+2` and
`l+1`; subtracting their allowed multiplicity degrees leaves exactly three
in both cases. Reflected companions use equality of canonical coordinates
at reflection-fixed readout indices. The proof does not assume reflection
invariance of the raw web or of its multiplicity polynomial.

The new source ledger and semantic review make the literature/proof boundary
accurately. Two narrow source descriptions were clarified in the ledger:
Du–Fu's block intervals and matrix bijection are located by §2, pp. 4–5,
and Vinroot's ordinary flag enumeration is distinguished from the elementary
invalid-rank extension used in the Lean interface. No mathematical source
correction was necessary.
