# Audit of the web argument, Sections 2–8

Audit date: 29 October 2026. Line anchors refer to the supplied
`PolishedWebProof/main.tex` (6148 lines). This is an independent mathematical
reading plus fresh finite arithmetic checks, not certification of the theorem.
No manuscript or prior research file was changed.

## Outcome and status distinctions

I found **no disproved identity or counterexample to the KL row-sum theorem**
in Sections 2–8. A recheck of W1 found that the manuscript already defines
the six required maps integrally as Hecke-module maps. An equivalence of
independent annular presentations is therefore not needed for that composite.
The earlier description of W1 as a missing proof justification was too strong;
it is now recorded as a possible ambiguity of exposition. Most of the
algebraic proof remains **unformalized**; that is not evidence of a paper gap.
See [gaps.md](gaps.md) for the corrected assessment.

## W1. Clarify what the annular pictures denote

**Status after recheck:** optional presentation clarification, not an
established gap in the algebraic proof.

**Anchors:** lines 3285–3306 (annular construction and coils), 3482–3487
(thin Bernstein relations), 3533–3544 (thick determinant dots and rotation),
3599–3609 (SSW identification), and 3904–3912 (integral cyclic vertex maps).

The manuscript first describes upward symmetric annular webs over
`Z[v,v^-1]` using disk relations and coils, then invokes SSW's integral
affine-web results. If this is read as an assertion that an independently
presented annular category is equivalent to SSW's, that additional categorical
claim needs its own comparison. SSW explicitly distinguishes:

- the integral category with a winding generator on every thick edge and
  relations (2.7)–(2.9), Definition 2.1, pp. 6–7;
- a simpler category generated by ordinary webs and thin affine-Hecke dots,
  Definition 2.12, pp. 13–14; its equivalence with the first category is
  proved **over C(q)** in Theorem 2.13. The reconstruction of a thick dot
  there divides an exploded diagram by `[a]!`, equation (2.34).

Source: [Shen–Song–Wang, arXiv:2504.10270v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10270v1),
also the supplied `RevisedWeb/references/ssw.txt`.
LTV Definition 4A.1, pp. 7–8, specifies coils and their relations for a
fixed-rank **exterior** annular category; its statement alone does not identify
the unrestricted integral symmetric presentation here with SSW.
Source: [Lacabanne–Tubbenhauer–Vaz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00947), checked
in the supplied `annular_levi.pdf`.

However, lines 3385–3409 already define integral inclusion and merge maps
for every finite parabolic inclusion `L ⊂ P`. The cyclic parabolic `J`
in lines 3884–3909 is finite, so it is covered. Over `Q(v)`, the merge
satisfies `mu_(L,P)(z)=C_P*z/h_L`, since `C_P*C_L=h_L*C_P`.
Composing the six maps therefore gives exactly

```
C_W0 -> C_V*C_K*C_J*C_W0 / (h_V*h_L^2).
```

This is the expression at lines 3935–3949. Its integrality follows from
the original module maps, before writing a denominator. Rotation also has
an integral realization `z -> tau^a*z` from `M_P` to
`M_(tau^a P tau^-a)`. The argument does not need the generic thin-dot
presentation equivalence. The manuscript actually cites SSW's integral
Theorem 4.16 and Proposition 4.18(1).

A useful clarification would say that the pictures denote these specified
integral maps, or that the working category is SSW's integral category and
annular drawings are notation for its morphisms. Only a stronger independent
presentation theorem would require a full comparison of thick relations.
The generic/integral distinction is a caution for that additional theorem,
not a demonstrated obstruction to the coefficient calculation here.

## W2. The Hecke-to-count and count-to-canonical bridges are not arithmetic definitions

**Status:** unformalized mathematical dependencies, rather than a new
disproof or an assertion that the paper's explanations are invalid.

**Anchors:** lines 3664–3712 (the ordinary KL/Schur dictionary), 3933–3967
(six maps and inverse-permutation convention), 4080–4151 (lattice galleries,
support exhaustion, and the finite flag bijection), 4343–4379 (canonical
positivity and support), 4667–4695 (one-line canonicality), 4834–4844
(full-spherical multiplicities), 4945–4959 (canonical refinement), and
5169–5194 (descending correction).

A formal theorem about the displayed Gaussian formula is not yet a theorem
about `P_{w0,C}`. The required bridges include:

1. Integral split/merge maps produce the stated symmetrizer expression, with
   the inverse-permutation anti-involution preserving normalized standard
   coefficients. This anti-involution is not multiplicative inversion.
2. Its standard coefficients equal the finite gallery count for every
   supported matrix. The lattice inclusions exclude all other relative
   exponents; equal determinant degree forces equally many `+1` and `-1`.
3. The canonical transition matrix is triangular and positive, the numerator
   has positive canonical structure constants, and the bar fixes the product.
4. The auxiliary products are the named canonical elements, refinement
   preserves them with the indicated powers of `v`, and all desired
   multiplicities are one.

The written arguments for these steps survived this reading. They should be
represented as proved theorems or explicitly declared hypotheses in any
partial formalization, not silently built into definitions of the numbers
called KL coefficients.

## W3. Degree and support checks: no error found, but feasibility is essential

**Anchors:** lines 4295–4341 (matrix parameters, count, and length),
4423–4527 (six-family elimination), 4600–4628 and 4667–4695 (one-line
parameters and canonicality), 5006–5057 (multiplicity-one margins).

Fresh code, independent of the supplied project scripts, computed the
periodic-matrix dimension sum directly and compared it with the proposed
length polynomial. It also derived the degree of the four *nonempty* count
terms directly from quantum integers and Gaussian degrees:

- all 29,153 feasible aggregate matrices for `2 <= m <= 20` passed the
  length identity and six-family exclusion; 29,058 had nonzero counts;
- all 9,486 feasible one-line matrices for `3 <= N <= 16` passed the length
  identity and nonpositive-excess test. Each allowed `(N,j,f)` had exactly
  one degree-zero standard: `(r,a,b)=(j,1,0)` for `f>j`, and `(j,1,1)` for
  `f=j`.

These are finite checks, not universal proofs. The quadratic inequalities
in the text also check algebraically with their feasibility conditions.
They are suitable targets for a genuine universal `omega`/`nlinarith`
formalization, separate from identifying polynomial degrees or canonical
support.

**Follow-up formalization:** `Guralnick/DegreeBounds.lean` now proves all
three uniform `l>=1` arithmetic exclusions, their combined theorem
`six_positions_of_bounds`, and the `l=0` theorem
`boundary_position_of_bounds`. The three degree-bound formulas are explicit
hypotheses; they are not disguised definitions of canonical support. The
proofs use `omega` and `nlinarith`, with no `sorry`, `admit`, extra declared
axioms, or `native_decide`. This upgrades the quadratic-exclusion step from
finite testing to a universal theorem, while leaving the bridges in W2 open.
Both `lake env lean Guralnick/DegreeBounds.lean` and
`lake build Guralnick.DegreeBounds` succeeded. `#print axioms` on the three
case theorems and the two combined/boundary theorems reports only Lean's
standard `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

In particular, a degree formula for a nonempty factor must not be applied
to the zero polynomial: `deg([a]_q)=a-1` requires `a>0`; a Gaussian requires
`0 <= k <= n`; and a difference `[c]_q-[a]_q` is nonzero only when `c>a`.
The manuscript handles these as empty terms. A formal version must retain
those branches.

## W4. The positivity-after-division argument is valid as written

**Status:** checked argument; explicit guard against a false strengthening.

**Anchors:** lines 4235–4269 and 4348–4379.

The nonnegative identity is `d*f_A = d*M_A + sum (d*M_Z)*h_AZ`.
It gives `val(d*M_A) >= val(d*f_A)`. Since minimum degrees add for nonzero
Laurent polynomials over `Z`, cancelling `val(d)` is valid even if `M_A`
has negative interior coefficients. Bar invariance then gives the upper
degree bound. A zero raw coefficient also forces the corresponding
canonical coefficient to vanish. No quotient-positivity assumption is used.

A useful formal regression counterexample is

```
d = v + v^-1
g = v^2 - 1 + v^-2
d*g = v^3 + v^-3.
```

Both `d` and `d*g` are nonnegative and bar invariant, but `g` has a negative
coefficient. The manuscript does **not** make the disproved inference that
`g` must therefore be coefficientwise nonnegative.

## W5. Gaussian cancellation: no error found; negative lower indices matter

**Anchors:** lines 5087–5149 and 5170–5194.

I checked the reciprocal exponent and the endpoint `H_1=0`. For
`l=m-r`, reversal gives the stated factor `q^r H_l Delta_r`, because

```
(m^2+2*m-3) - (l^2+2*l-3) - r*(2*m+1-r) = r.
```

The truncation defect at degree `m-1` is `choose(m-r+1,2)`; multiplication
by `Delta_r` retains that defect only for `r=0`. The finite telescoping
congruence and the Euler partition-series identity then give the asserted
coefficient. A fresh Gaussian recursion independently verified the exact
degree and leading coefficient of `Q_m` for `2 <= m <= 16`.

**Concrete Lean pitfall:** the polynomial convention has
`Gaussian(n,-1)=0`. Literal natural subtraction turns `h-1` into `0` at
`h=0`, which would replace the correct `R_(n,0)=1` by the false expression
`1-q^(n+1)`. Use an integer lower index or an explicit zero branch. Likewise
handle the declared `H_1=0` separately from formulas involving `2*l-3`.

The descending correction is not circular: at a full-spherical standard,
one-line contributions begin in degree at least three, and proper desired
canonical coefficients have positive degree with constant multiplicities.
Their nonpositive parts vanish. Bar symmetry therefore determines the
remaining full-spherical multiplicity from its nonpositive part.

## Limits of this audit

isotopy or rendering audit was performed. The cohomology section and its
external transfer theorems are outside this Sections 2–8 audit. The finite
checks do not establish a uniform KL theorem. No result here relies on
