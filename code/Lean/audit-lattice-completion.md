## The four general lattice facts

The four facts packaged in `LaurentLattice.Theory` are now proved, and
`LaurentLattice.provedTheory` constructs that bundle. The
`GeneralLatticeFlagInput.lattice_theory` field has been removed from the
external input. Its compatibility method returns the proved bundle and
uses no property of its retained argument. The remaining finite-flag
counting formulas are still an external input.

1. **One period.** A finite torsion-free module over the power-series PID
   has a finite basis. Its scalar extension is independent and spans the
   actual Laurent ambient space, proving that the basis has N elements.
   For x in t⁻¹Λ, take the constant coefficients of the lattice-basis
   coordinates of tx. The map is onto k^N and has kernel exactly Λ.
   Thus `periodEquiv` is an actual linear equivalence, proving both
   `period_finite` and `period_dimension`. The desired dimension is not
   included in a definition or a new premise.
2. **Relative quotients.** Fullness and Laurent denominator clearing send
   each vector into Λ after multiplication by a power of t. Finite
   generation supplies one exponent working for an entire lattice M.
   The iterated actual expanded lattices then contain M. Their relative
   quotients are finite-dimensional by successive one-period quotients,
   and M/(M∩Λ) embeds into such a quotient. This proves `finite_quotient`
   with exactly the nested-lattice statement used by the project.
3. **Standard lattice and intersections.** The actual coordinate module
   is finitely generated and has full Laurent span. Intersections of two
   lattices are finitely generated by Noetherianity; simultaneous
   denominator clearing proves they still span. These are genuine
   lattice constructors, not assumed dimension realizations.
4. **Signed degree.** With S the actual standard lattice, put I=Λ∩S and
   J=M∩S. For Λ⊆M, quotient-dimension additivity in I⊆Λ⊆M,
   I⊆J⊆M, and I⊆J⊆S gives
   `dim(M/Λ)=degree(M)−degree(Λ)`. All required finite-dimensionality
   comes from the constructed intersections and proved relative quotients.

These proofs apply over every field and include ambient dimension zero.
The pre-existing one-period interval lift is retained as a proved theorem.

## The natural finite completion map

`PowerSeriesCompletion.completionEquiv` is the actual map induced by
polynomial inclusion, giving k[t]/(t^d) ≃ k[[t]]/(t^d). Its formula on
representatives is proved. Injectivity compares coefficients below d;
surjectivity uses the actual polynomial truncation. Multiplication by t
is preserved, as are arbitrary ring operations and the k-algebra structure.
The modulus d=0 is included.

`CompletionIncidence` applies this map coordinatewise and constructs an
order isomorphism on actual submodules. It preserves intersection, sum,
stability under t, subspace dimension and the dimension of ambient
quotients. The finite quotient dimension d follows from the polynomial
power basis. These are structural isomorphisms, not equality of numerical
counts followed by an asserted interpretation.

## Actual bounded lattice intervals

`LatticeTruncatedLift` addresses a different necessary step. For any actual
Λ and d, a k-subspace A satisfying Λ⊆A⊆t⁻ᵈΛ is the underlying subspace of
a full lattice exactly when it is stable under t. To prove stability under
an arbitrary power series, split it into its polynomial truncation and a
t^d multiple. The polynomial part preserves A by t-stability; the tail
lands in Λ. Inclusion in the actual upper lattice proves finite generation,
and containment of Λ proves full span.

`truncatedIntervalIso` gives the exact order correspondence. At d=0 the
construction returns Λ. At d=1 parameter stability is automatic and the
construction agrees with the earlier one-period lift.

## What remained at this stage (now superseded)

The finite quotient comparison and the bounded actual-lattice
correspondence are proved separately. A full polynomial/completed lattice
comparison still needs their assembly: identify both models with the same
finite quotient, construct the polynomial lattice inverse images, and prove
compatibility when bounds change and under Laurent shifts. The statement
about signed lattice degree proved above is not, by itself, a theorem that
completion preserves that degree between two lattice models.

The general affine flag/Hecke convolution interface also still requires
complete-to-partial flag fibers for cyclic masks and the balanced affine
component, with absolute degrees respected. None of those interfaces is
claimed to follow just from the new truncation theorem. The geometric
convolution, orbit-coordinate and representation-theoretic inputs remain
explicit external hypotheses. The main counterexample is still conditional.

The source follow-up in `audit-andersen-followup.md` identifies original
Andersen 1986 §2, Propositions 2.8 and 2.12, more precisely. Accessible
quantum and category O analogues do not replace the required modular
fixed-trivial-source theorem; the original full-text check remains open.
No theorem from those newly consulted papers was added as a Lean assumption.
